Spider-Man stays: Jacob Young locked in through 2030

Welcome back

Issue #6! The GM Meetings came and went in Las Vegas, the Nats handed out their second extension in two months, and James Wood picked up some hardware. This week: the Young deal, our first CBA Watch, a Rule 5 check-in, Prospect Spotlight (Seaver King and Jarlin Susana), Fall League playoffs, and a couple of X posts worth a chuckle.

Stats from the MLB Stats API (through Nov. 12). Sources are linked. Anything marked Our take is opinion.

Lead story: Spider-Man signs on

On Nov. 11 the Nationals finalized a four-year extension with center fielder Jacob Young. It starts in 2027, runs through 2030, covers three of his arbitration years, and has a 2031 club option. MLB.com’s Mark Feinsand reports it guarantees $23 million, with the option worth $8 million (Nationals release, MLB.com, ESPN).

  • The bat grew up: career highs in doubles (22), triples (3), homers (10), RBI (50) and walks (33). He had hit just 5 homers in his first 1,006 big-league plate appearances, then hit 10 in 471 this year. From July 1 on he hit .301/.390/.428.
  • The glove is still the glove: 11 outs above average in 2026 (third among NL center fielders), and second among all MLB outfielders over the last three seasons with 46 OAA and 41 four-star catches, per Statcast via the club.
  • Clutch, too: .405 (17-for-42) with two outs and runners in scoring position, second-best in baseball.

It is the second extension under Paul Toboni, after Cade Cavalli’s four-year, $47 million deal in September. Toboni also told reporters at the GM Meetings the offseason will “be mostly about pitching,” with Cavalli, Connelly Early and Andrew Alvarez penciled into the rotation and Jake Irvin, Jackson Kent and DJ Herz competing for the last spots (The Banner).

Our take: About $5.75 million a year for an elite center fielder whose bat just woke up is a bargain, and it gives the young core a defensive anchor. The bigger question it raises is where Dylan Crews fits in the outfield long term (Federal Baseball explored it).

Hardware and headlines

  • Wood is All-MLB: James Wood made the 2026 All-MLB Second Team, the Nats’ first All-MLB pick since 2021. He hit .265/.388/.503 with 30 HR, 21 SB and 102 walks in 135 games, and MLB.com says he is the first player ever with 30 homers, 20 steals and 100 walks in under 140 games. He and Adam Dunn are the only Washington-era Nats with back-to-back 30-homer seasons (MLB.com). Silver Slugger winners come Nov. 17, and Wood and CJ Abrams are both finalists.
  • Lefty for the pen: On Nov. 6 the Nats got LHP Reiver Sanmartin from the Giants for Minor League RHP Robert Cranz. Sanmartin, 30, had a 4.22 ERA in 24 relief outings and a 63.8% ground-ball rate, second in MLB among pitchers with 20+ innings, plus a 1.69 ERA over his last 19 games. RHP Kyle Nicolas was designated to make room and released Nov. 12 (MLB.com, Nats transactions).
  • Also: Cleveland claimed José Tena off waivers on Nov. 6. Nats Talk reports the club has checked in on old friend Foster Griffin, though nothing is close (@TalkNats).
  • Remembering Bob Boone: The longtime Nationals front-office executive and seven-time Gold Glove catcher died Nov. 9 at 78 (MLB.com, @Nationals).

Our take: The bullpen finished with the third-highest ERA in baseball (4.99), so a ground-ball lefty for the cost of a Minor Leaguer is a sensible first step. It is not the big swing, though.

CBA Watch Weekly · CBA expires Dec. 1

Where it stands: The current deal expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on Dec. 1, and owners are widely expected to lock players out right away. A lockout freezes big-league signings and trades, but it does not cancel games by itself. The last one ran 99 days and ended in March 2022 (NBC Sports Philadelphia tracker).

  • This week: No new bargaining session was reported. The league’s latest offer is still the Nov. 3 one: a $258.2M salary cap and $180.2M floor starting in 2028, with the current luxury tax and revenue sharing staying for 2027. The union rejected it and still calls any cap a nonstarter (MLB.com, MLBPA).
  • New wrinkle: The Wall Street Journal reports MLB is preparing for the union to file a “disclaimer of interest” during a lockout. That means temporarily stepping down as a union so players could sue the league on antitrust grounds instead of bargaining. One expert called it “the nuclear option” (MLB Trade Rumors, Nov. 12).
  • Other big issues on the table: a higher minimum salary ($1M with two-plus years of service), a bigger pre-arbitration bonus pool, free agency after five years for players 30 and older, fewer option years, and Rule 5 eligibility one year earlier (NBC Sports Philadelphia).

What it means for the Nats: Washington is one of 12 clubs that would have to raise payroll to reach MLB’s proposed floor, while eight clubs, including the Mets and Phillies, would have to cut to get under the cap (MLB.com). In the short term, any pitching Toboni wants has to be done in the next 18 days, because the 40-man roster freezes once a lockout starts.

Our take: Expect a busy two weeks around the league and then a long, quiet winter.

Rule 5 check-in Follow-up to Issue #5

No protections announced yet. Clubs have until Tuesday, Nov. 17 at 6 p.m. ET to add players to the 40-man. One correction from Issue #5: MLB.com now lists the Rule 5 Draft for Wednesday, Dec. 9 in San Diego, not Dec. 14 (MLB.com).

MLB.com lists Travis Sykora (No. 48 overall) and Jarlin Susana (No. 66) among only eight Top 100 prospects across baseball who must be added or be exposed. The 40-man is full at 40 after the Sanmartin trade, so at least two spots have to open by Tuesday. We will report who actually got protected next week.

Prospect Spotlight Week 2 · #3 and #4

Walking MLB Pipeline’s current Nationals Top 30 in order, two per week. Done so far: #1 Eli Willits and #2 Travis Sykora.

#3Seaver King, SS/2B

Age 23 · Harrisburg (AA) → Rochester (AAA) · 112 G · .303/.382/.516 · 21 HR · 15 SB

2026: After hitting .244 in 2025, the 2024 first-rounder (No. 10 overall, Wake Forest) went off. At Double-A Harrisburg he hit .336/.427/.562 with 5 HR and 22 walks against 31 strikeouts in 35 games. He was promoted May 18 and hit .289/.361/.497 with 16 HR and 10 SB in 77 games for Triple-A Rochester (MLB Stats API). He was named the Nats’ 2026 Minor League Hitter of the Year and helped Rochester win the International League title (The Nats Report (Substack)).

Tools and strengths: elite bat speed and athleticism. Baseball America says his 50.5% hard-hit rate ranked in the 95th percentile of pro hitters, and it grades his run and arm both at 60 (plus). He also lifted the ball more, cutting his ground-ball rate from about 52% to about 45%, which is how 6 homers became 21 (Baseball America, Federal Baseball).

What needs work: plate discipline. His chase rate climbed back into the mid-30s at Triple-A after a patient start in Double-A, and his walk rate fell. He made 10 errors in 30 Double-A games at short, and BA projects him at second or third base. His stolen-base total also dropped from 30 in 2025.

ETA: 2027, per Baseball America. Our take: He looks like a big-league regular next year if the swing decisions hold up. Second base looks like the cleanest fit.

#4Jarlin Susana, RHP

Age 22 · 6'6", 235 · A+/AA/AAA · 8 GS · 20.1 IP · 37 K · 11 BB · AFL Fall Star

2026: Lat surgery late in 2025 delayed his season debut until Aug. 11 (MLB.com). Once back he made 3 starts at High-A Wilmington (6.1 IP, 13 K), 4 at Double-A Harrisburg (11.1 IP, 4.76 ERA, 21 K, 4 BB), and one at Triple-A Rochester (2.2 IP). In the Arizona Fall League he has a 3.86 ERA in 6 starts, 26 K in 18.2 IP, and a .162 opponents’ average (MLB Stats API). He was a Fall Star.

Tools and strengths: pure gas. His four-seamer and sinker both sit in the triple digits. He touched 102.9 mph on Oct. 27 and was still at 102.0 on his last pitch of a five-inning start. He pairs it with an 85-88 mph power curveball that got 5 whiffs on 6 swings that day (MLB.com). Federal Baseball notes he has thrown the nine hardest pitches in AFL history (Federal Baseball).

What needs work: strike-throwing and innings. He walked 13 in 18.2 AFL innings, and that five-inning start was his first since Aug. 2025.

ETA: No firm date from the sources. Our take: If he stays healthy, the stuff could help the big-league bullpen sometime in 2027 while the club keeps trying to build him up as a starter. First he needs his spot on the 40-man by Tuesday.

Ranks: MLB Pipeline Nationals Top 30 (checked Nov. 13). Next week: #5 Devin Fitz-Gerald and #6 Chris Hacopian.

The NL East group chat Light ribbing, real numbers

Mets: Pete Crow-Armstrong won the NL MVP this week, and it was unanimous (all 30 first-place votes, BBWAA). He was a Mets prospect until 2021, when they traded him to the Cubs for Javier Báez and Trevor Williams. Meanwhile the Mets, who finished last in the East at 74-88, are among six teams lining up to meet with Tarik Skubal (AP).

Phillies: USA Today’s Bob Nightengale reports Philadelphia has made Trea Turner and Brandon Marsh available in trade talks (SI). And under MLB’s cap proposal, both the Mets and Phillies are on the list of clubs that would have to shed payroll.

Our take: The Nats are extending homegrown players while Queens and Philly try to cut spending or shop their stars. We will take that trade. 😄
