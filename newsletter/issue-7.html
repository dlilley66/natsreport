Issue #7Week of Nov 20, 2026

Gray day: Nats protect Susana, Sykora and Rosario, say goodbye to Josiah Gray

Welcome back

Issue #7! Rule 5 protection day came and went, and the Nats protected three young arms. To make room they said goodbye to a fan favorite. James Wood also added a Silver Slugger to his shelf, Mr. National made the Hall of Fame ballot, and the labor fight got louder. This week: the 40-man shuffle, the rest of the week’s news, CBA Watch, Prospect Spotlight (Devin Fitz-Gerald and Chris Hacopian), the end of Fall League, and a few X posts worth a chuckle.

Stats from the MLB Stats API (through Nov. 19). Sources are linked. Anything marked Our take is opinion.

Lead story: Protect the arms, say goodbye to Josiah

Tuesday, Nov. 17 was the Rule 5 protection deadline (6 p.m. ET), and Washington made eight moves in one afternoon. It selected the contracts of right-handers Jarlin Susana, Travis Sykora and Alejandro Rosario, and it claimed lefty Philip Abner off waivers from Baltimore. To clear four spots it designated four pitchers for assignment: Josiah Gray, Braxton Roxby, Gus Varland and Carson Palmquist (Nationals release, MLB.com, MLBTR).

  • The tough one: Gray came over in the July 2021 Max Scherzer/Trea Turner trade, made the 2023 All-Star team and got an Opening Day start. With Washington he went 17-27 with a 4.75 ERA in 75 games (72 starts) over 382 2/3 innings. Tommy John surgery in 2024 and a setback this spring kept him out until September. He came back as a reliever and earned his first career save at Nationals Park on Sept. 25 (MLB.com, Nats Journal, Washington Post).
  • Why it surprised people: Talk Nats points out that MLB Trade Rumors projected Gray’s 2027 arbitration salary at only $1.6 million. He was a cheap tender, but the club needed the roster spot (Talk Nats).
  • Toboni’s yardstick: “How likely we think they’re going to impact our team this year — or, honestly, another team,” Paul Toboni said Monday, plus whether a player would stick in the majors if picked and fit the clubhouse (MLB.com).
  • The others: Varland had a 6.55 ERA and six saves in Washington this year. Palmquist made six starts as an opener with a 7.71 ERA. Roxby was DFA’d less than a month after the Nats claimed him from the Giants. Abner has a 6.75 ERA in 18 big-league games with Arizona and San Francisco, but he held Triple-A lefties to a .158 average last year (MLB.com).

The 40-man roster is full again at 40 (Nats 40-man).

Our take: It’s the right baseball call. Losing Susana or Sykora for nothing would hurt for years. It still stings, though. Gray did everything asked of him, including years of work as the Nationals Youth Baseball Academy’s player ambassador. We hope he lands somewhere good.

Rule 5 follow-up Follow-up to Issue #6

Last week we said no protections had been announced. Here is the final answer. Protected (added to the 40-man):

  • Travis Sykora, RHP: Nats No. 2, MLB No. 48. He has a 2.14 ERA, a .152 opponents’ average and 208 strikeouts in 130 1/3 career innings, and he is coming back from UCL reconstruction.
  • Jarlin Susana, RHP: Nats No. 4, MLB No. 66. Since 2023 he leads all minor leaguers (min. 240 IP) in strikeouts per nine (12.98) and fewest homers per nine (0.33).
  • Alejandro Rosario, RHP: Nats No. 19, part of the MacKenzie Gore trade. He is recovering from Tommy John surgery and hasn’t pitched in a game since 2024.

Stats from the Nationals release and MLB.com.

Left unprotected (any of them could be picked if the Dec. 9 draft happens): OF Yeremy Cabrera (No. 27, the only Top 30 Nat left off), 3B Cayden Wallace (.300/.353/.543 and 29 HR at Double-A and Triple-A), C Maxwell Romero Jr., OF Elijah Green, OF Cristhian Vaquero, INF Trey Lipscomb and 3B Jorgelys Mota (MLB.com). MLB.com notes the Dec. 9 Rule 5 Draft in San Diego comes a week after the CBA expires and “could be impacted” without a deal.

Our take: Wallace and Romero are the two most likely to get picked, if anyone does. With a lockout coming, the bigger question may be whether there is a draft at all.

The week’s other news

  • Jimmy Lumber gets his silver: James Wood won his first Silver Slugger as an NL outfielder on Nov. 17. He led NL outfielders in OBP (.388) and walks (102) and was second in homers (30), runs (106) and OPS (.891), even after missing 27 games. He also led MLB in average exit velocity (95.4 mph). He is the first Nat to win since Juan Soto in 2021 (Nationals release). CJ Abrams (33 HR, 106 RBI) lost the NL shortstop award to Elly De La Cruz, who had the higher slugging percentage and OPS (Nats Journal).
  • Mr. National on the ballot: Ryan Zimmerman is one of 14 first-time candidates on the 2027 BBWAA Hall of Fame ballot. He was the first draft pick in Nationals history (No. 4 in 2005), hit 11 walk-off homers and finished with 40.1 career WAR. Federal Baseball doesn’t expect him to get in, but it hopes he clears the 5% needed to stay on the ballot (MLB.com, Federal Baseball).
  • A $380 million facelift? On Nov. 13 the team asked the D.C. Council to approve bonds for a $380 million Nationals Park renovation. The money would go to concourses, concessions, premium seating, new seating areas and player areas. The bill allows up to $410 million in borrowing, repaid from a ballpark fund fed by ballpark taxes and rent. A council vote could come as early as Dec. 1 (Axios, WJLA).
  • Ford, the ambassador: Harry Ford hosted an invite-only clinic in England for about 85 participants, including Great Britain youth national team players on Nov. 7–8 (MLB.com).
  • Depth signing: RHP Hagen Danner, 28, a former catcher, signed a minor league deal. He had a 6.35 ERA at Triple-A in 2026 (MLBTR).
  • Today: tender day. Clubs must offer 2027 contracts to arbitration-eligible players by 5 p.m. ET Friday (4 p.m. CT) (NBC Sports Philadelphia). Only three Nats decisions are left: CJ Abrams (projected $10.2M, a sure thing), Jake Irvin (projected $3.4M after a 5.48 ERA) and Jake Bird (projected $1.3M, out of options) (Talk Nats, using MLBTR projections). We will have the results next week.

CBA Watch Weekly · CBA expires Dec. 1, 11:59 p.m. ET

Where it stands: It got louder this week but no closer. The deal expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on Dec. 1, and the AP says baseball is “on track for a lockout starting Dec. 2.” That would be the sport’s 10th work stoppage since 1972, and it would halt all trades and signings (AP).

  • Owners dig in (Nov. 18): After the owners meetings, Commissioner Rob Manfred said the 30 clubs are “100% unanimous” behind the cap-and-floor plan, and he “can’t imagine the owners ratifying a deal that does not include a cap and floor.” He said the lockout decision will be made “at the last possible moment,” and that MLB is willing to modify the system to address the union’s criticisms (MLB.com).
  • Union fires back (Nov. 17): MLBPA interim executive director Bruce Meyer told the Boston Globe the league has “wasted months of time” by refusing to talk economics without a cap. He said there has been progress on non-money issues like drug testing and gambling policy. The union’s board meets in Scottsdale at the end of November (Boston Globe, CBS timeline).
  • Congress wades in (Nov. 19): Sens. Dick Durbin (D-Ill.) and Mike Lee (R-Utah) introduced a bill to repeal baseball’s 100-year-old antitrust exemption, and the players’ union backed it (Senate Judiciary, AFP).
  • Still the key issues: MLB’s offer is unchanged since Nov. 3: a $258.2M cap and $180.2M floor starting in 2028, with today’s luxury tax and revenue sharing staying for 2027. The union calls a cap a nonstarter. Service-time items are also on the table, including free agency after five years for players 30 and older, fewer option years, and Rule 5 eligibility a year earlier (see Issue #6).

What it means for the Nats: Washington is one of 12 clubs that would have to raise payroll to reach the proposed floor. Those 12 would need a combined $636 million more (MLB.com). Once a lockout starts, the 40-man roster is frozen, with no big-league trades or signings, so Toboni has about 11 days for any pitching moves. Players on the 40-man, now including Susana, Sykora and Rosario, can’t play in the minors during a shutdown (NBC Sports Philadelphia explains the rule).

Our take: A lockout on Dec. 2 looks all but certain. The Boston Globe reports neither side is expected to make concessions before February at the earliest. Plan on a quiet winter.

Prospect Spotlight Week 3 · #5 and #6

Walking MLB Pipeline’s current Nationals Top 30 in order, two per week. Done so far: #1 Eli Willits, #2 Travis Sykora, #3 Seaver King, #4 Jarlin Susana.

#5Devin Fitz-Gerald, INF

Age 21 · Switch-hitter · Wilmington (A+) → Harrisburg (AA) · 114 G · .251/.339/.441 · 20 HR · 15 SB · ETA 2028

Who he is: He came over from Texas in January’s MacKenzie Gore trade. The Rangers took him in the fifth round in 2024 out of Stoneman Douglas High in Parkland, Fla., where his dad, Todd, is the head coach (the same program produced Anthony Rizzo and Roman Anthony), and paid him a $900,000 bonus (Baseball America).

2026: A tale of two levels. At High-A Wilmington he hit .267/.373/.530 with 12 HR and 11 SB in 51 games, with 30 walks and 42 strikeouts. After a June jump to Double-A Harrisburg he hit .237/.309/.369 with 8 HR in 63 games, and his strikeout rate rose from about 18% to about 26% (our math from MiLB.com stats). BA says he chased more during his slump, then got back to fewer chases, more walks and fewer strikeouts in August and September after a stint on the injured list.

Tools and strengths: a coach’s-kid feel for the game. MLB Pipeline likes his barrel feel and says scouts prefer his left-handed swing, projecting 12-15 homers a year. BA says he “consistently backspins the ball” to his pull side, so his power plays as average in games. BA grades: Hit 55, Power 50 (MLB Pipeline, BA).

What needs work: pitch selection against Double-A arms, plus defense and speed. He made 10 errors at Harrisburg, split between second and short (MLB Stats API). BA grades him Run 40, Field 45, Arm 45 and projects a fringe-average defender at second base.

ETA: 2028 (MLB Pipeline). BA sees him most likely as a “bat-first everyday second baseman.” Our take: The strong finish matters. If he starts 2027 back at Harrisburg and hits like he did in August and September, he moves fast.

#6Chris Hacopian, 2B

Age 22 · Bats R · 2026 No. 11 overall pick (Texas A&M) · Wilmington (A+) · 13 G · .275/.396/.350 · 1 HR · 8 BB, 8 K · ETA 2029

Who he is: a hometown kid. He was born in Potomac, Md., and played at Churchill High (BA). His dad, Derek, won the ACC Triple Crown at Maryland in 1992. Chris hit .375/.502/.656 with 14 homers for the Terps in 2025, with 40 walks against 19 strikeouts, then transferred to Texas A&M (MLB Pipeline).

2026: He hit .319/.405/.578 with 11 HR for the Aggies despite a lower-back injury that cost him nearly a month. The Nats took him 11th overall and signed him for $5.7 million. In his High-A debut he hit .275/.396/.350 with 8 walks and 8 strikeouts in 13 games (MiLB.com).

Tools and strengths: the bat. Some clubs thought he had the best bat in the college class (MLB Pipeline). His career college strikeout rate was just 9.3%, and BA says his in-zone miss rate in pro ball was lower than 98% of High-A hitters. Hit grade: 60 from both MLB Pipeline and BA.

What needs work: game power and glove. His swing is built for line drives to the opposite field. BA calls him a “stiff mover” with below-average speed, and he has limited range at second. MLB Pipeline says he could end up in left field. The back also needs to stay healthy.

ETA: 2029 (MLB Pipeline). BA sees “all-star upside if he unlocks more game power.” Our take: Pure hitters with this kind of zone control don’t usually take long in the minors. A local kid hitting his way to Nats Park in a few years would be a great story.

Ranks: MLB Pipeline Nationals Top 30 (checked Nov. 20). Next week: #7 Ronny Cruz and #8 Gavin Fien.
