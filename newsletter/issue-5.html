Protect or lose: the Rule 5 clock is ticking

Welcome back

Issue #5! Free agency is open, the 40-man got a haircut, and the biggest decision of November is which prospects get locked down before Nov. 17. This week: the roster crunch explained, a one-time Rule 5 preview, Prospect Spotlight (Travis Sykora and Eli Willits), Fall League heat, and a couple of X posts worth a smile.

Stats from the MLB Stats API (through Nov. 5). Sources are linked. Anything marked Our take is opinion.

Lead story: November already got loud

Paul Toboni did not wait around. On Nov. 1 the Nationals traded Minor League lefty Tony Neubeck to San Francisco for second baseman Nate Furman and designated lefty Richard Lovelady for assignment (MLB.com, MLB Trade Rumors).

  • Furman: hit .293 with a .791 OPS in 102 Triple-A games for Sacramento, stole 19 of 24, and debuted in the big leagues (.125 in 39 PA). He has three options left.
  • Nov. 2 crunch: clubs had to reinstate 60-day IL players. The Nats activated Drew Millas and Matt Waldron, designated José Tena, PJ Poulin and Ken Waldichuk for assignment, and outrighted Poulin, Waldichuk and Lovelady to Rochester. Atlanta claimed Mitchell Parker; Cincinnati claimed Paxton Schultz (Nats transactions, MLB.com injuries/moves).
  • Also this week: the club released Zak Kent after last week’s DFA (MLBTR). Daylen Lile was a Gold Glove finalist in left field but finished behind Milwaukee’s Jackson Chourio (MLB.com). On the bright side, CJ Abrams and James Wood are Silver Slugger finalists, and the Nats are up for the team Silver Slugger (@Nationals).

Free agency is open now, but the CBA expires Dec. 1 / early Dec. 2, and a lockout would freeze deals. Toboni’s November window is short (Nats Journal, Washington Post).

Our take: Furman is a low-cost, high-OBP utility bet with options. Losing Parker and Schultz cleared IL/40-man math more than it hurt the 2027 bullpen plan. The real test is still the Rule 5 deadline below.

Rule 5 Watch One-time · Nov 17 / Dec 14

Clubs must set the 40-man by Nov. 17 to protect Rule 5–eligible players. The Rule 5 Draft itself is scheduled for Dec. 14 (during Winter Meetings week), though a lockout could scrub or delay it. Either way, the protection deadline still matters (Nats Journal).

How eligibility works (quick): players signed at 19 or older become Rule 5 eligible after four seasons; those signed younger get five. That’s why some 2023 high-school arms are due now and others are not.

  • Locks (consensus): RHP Travis Sykora (Pipeline #2) and RHP Jarlin Susana (Pipeline #4). Sykora was a 2023 third-rounder who was 19 on the June 5 before signing, so he is eligible after four seasons even though most high-school draftees get five (FanGraphs RosterResource, MLBTR). Susana’s stuff is Fall League headline material (see below).
  • Likely: C Maxwell Romero Jr. — upper-level bat with power at Harrisburg/Rochester; Nats Journal and other roster guides list him with the must-protect group (Nats Journal).
  • Bubble: INF Cayden Wallace, RHP Alejandro Rosario, and other names that keep showing up on protection boards — Phillip Glasser, Tyler Stuart, Elijah Green (Fanrecap / roster crunch write-ups). Our take: Wallace’s Double-A power makes him the strongest bubble case; Green’s tools never fully translated and several write-ups already lean “leave unprotected.”
  • Not on this list: players with prior MLB time who were already outrighted (for example Trey Lipscomb) are not classic Rule 5 “add or lose” prospects the same way. We are leaving them off.

Protecting three locks means at least three more 40-man spots. After this week’s outrights and claims the roster is full again at 40, so expect more DFAs, non-tenders (Abrams, Jacob Young, Jake Irvin, Josiah Gray, Jake Bird, Parker and Waldichuk were among the arb group Nats Journal flagged), or a trade before Nov. 17. No protection adds have been announced yet as of Nov. 5.

Our take: Sykora and Susana are non-negotiable. Romero is next. Everything else is Toboni weighing risk vs. another bullpen arm on the 40-man.

Prospect Spotlight New · 2 per week

Starting this issue we will walk MLB Pipeline’s Nationals Top 30, two names a week, so the whole list is covered by mid-February. Covered players are tracked so we do not repeat. This week leads with a deep dive on Sykora, then the highest-ranked name still open: Willits.

#2Travis Sykora, RHP

Age 22 · Harrisburg (AA) · No 2026 innings (TJ rehab)

The injury: In late July 2025 the Nationals told reporters Sykora needed Tommy John surgery (right UCL reconstruction). He missed the rest of 2025 and all of 2026 on the mound; MLB.com lists expected return as 2027, with rehab continuing through the offseason (MLBTR, July 28, 2025, MLB.com injuries). Harrisburg activated him from the 60-day IL on Nov. 2 — an administrative step, not a pitching announcement.

Where he left off (2025, pre-surgery): 12 starts across levels up to Double-A, 45 1/3 IP, 1.79 ERA, walk rate a bit over 10%, strikeout rate around 46.7% per MLBTR’s write-up. In 2024 as a 20-year-old he threw 85 IP over 20 Single-A starts with a 2.33 ERA and a 39.2% K rate. Velocity was climbing back toward triple digits after a prior hip procedure before the elbow went (Federal Baseball).

Tools / what needs work: Mid-to-upper-90s fastball with swing-and-miss secondary stuff and starter traits when healthy. The work now is workload, command after a full missed year, and proving the stuff holds over a starter’s pitch count in 2027.

ETA / 40-man: 2027 contributor if the rehab sticks. Must be added to the 40-man by Nov. 17 or another club can take him in the Rule 5 Draft — the rare high-school arm who is eligible early because he signed at 19.

Our take: Protect him even while he is still building up. Stuff this loud does not stay unclaimed in December.

#1Eli Willits, SS

Age 18 · AA/A+/A · .265/.406/.463 · 14 HR · 62 SB

The 2025 No. 1 overall pick finished 2026 at Double-A Harrisburg after stops at Fredericksburg and Wilmington. Across the year he posted a high-OBP line with 14 homers and 60-plus steals (site line: .265/.406/.463, 14 HR, 62 SB; SI’s final Top 100 recap had him around .269/.405/.452 with 64 SB in 129 games) and remains a top-10 prospect in baseball (MLB Pipeline, SI).

Tools / what needs work: Plus hit and run tools, improving power, shortstop defense that projects to stick. The next step is proving the Double-A approach holds over a full 2027, and adding consistent impact against advanced pitching.

ETA / 40-man: Still years from Rule 5 pressure. Big-league ETA is mid-to-late decade; he is the franchise’s clearest long-term building block next to the current big-league core.

Our take: Best player in the system, and the stolen-base total fits the “young and fast” identity we keep bragging about to Philadelphia and Queens.

Ranks: MLB Pipeline Nationals Top 30 as reflected on the Nats Report. Next week: #3 Seaver King and #4 Jarlin Susana.
