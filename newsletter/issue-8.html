Issue #8Week of Nov 27, 2026

Puk drop: Nats grab a closer-caliber lefty before the lockout lights go out

Welcome back

Happy Thanksgiving week, and welcome to Issue #8! While the rest of us were basting turkeys, the Nats front office was working the phones. They landed a lefty with closer stuff just before the holiday, a Nats reliever won a big national award, and the labor fight ended up in federal court. This week: the A.J. Puk signing, the rest of the week’s news, CBA Watch (five days to go), Prospect Spotlight (Ronny Cruz and Gavin Fien), a winter ball check-in, some NL East ribbing, and a few X posts worth a chuckle.

Stats from the MLB Stats API (through Nov. 26). Sources are linked. Anything marked Our take is opinion.

Lead story: Puk drop in D.C.

Late on Thanksgiving Eve, the Nats agreed to terms with left-handed reliever A.J. Puk. The deal became official Thursday morning after he passed his physical. It’s a one-year, $5 million major league contract with up to $2.5 million in incentives and a conditional club option for 2028 (MLB.com, MLBTR, Nationals on X).

  • The upside: From 2022 to 2024, Puk had a 3.38 ERA with 242 strikeouts in 194 1/3 innings, and his 31.1% strikeout rate was tied for 19th among 185 qualified relievers. In 2024, his slider got a whiff on 47.8% of swings (MLB.com). After a deadline trade to Arizona that year, he had a 1.32 ERA with 43 strikeouts and 5 walks in 27 1/3 innings (MLB Stats API). Lefties hit just .189/.230/.320 against him from 2023 to 2025 (MLBTR).
  • The risk: He hasn’t pitched in a big-league game since April 17, 2025. He had internal brace surgery on his elbow in June 2025, then a sprained capsule in his throwing shoulder this June ended his 2026 season (MLBTR). The 2028 option, worth $2 million, kicks in only if he spends more than 60% of 2027 on the injured list with a shoulder injury, per Nats Journal and The Athletic (via MLBTR).
  • Why the Nats needed him: Washington’s bullpen blew 42 leads in 2026, the most in MLB history and 11 more than any other club. Only the A’s and Royals had a worse bullpen ERA than the Nats’ 4.99 (MLBTR). Talk Nats calls it the club’s first free-agent signing above the league minimum this offseason (Talk Nats).
  • The price check: Other relievers got a lot more this week. JoJo Romero got three years and $21 million from San Diego, and Kevin Ginkel got two years and $18 million from Minnesota (Talk Nats).
  • A family reunion: His younger brother, RHP Owen Puk, is already in the Nats’ system. He was an 18th-round pick in 2025 and pitched in three Arizona Fall League games this fall (Talk Nats, Talk Nats on X). Thanksgiving dinner at the Puk house just got a lot more Nats-friendly.

The Nats’ lefty relievers now include Puk, Reiver Sanmartin, Erik Tolman, Will Dion and Philip Abner (MLBTR). The 40-man roster is full at 40 (Nats 40-man).

Our take: This is the right kind of gamble. One year, modest money, and if his arm holds up, he could close. If not, the Nats aren’t stuck with a long contract. After 42 blown saves, an elite lefty, even a risky one, is exactly what this bullpen needs.

The week’s other news

  • Tolman wins the Tony C: Erik Tolman won the 2026 Tony Conigliaro Award, which honors a major leaguer who has overcome adversity. He had two Tommy John surgeries, then in 2023 a freak injury tore multiple ligaments in his knee and doctors discussed amputating his leg. He came back, debuted on Aug. 22, and finished with 5 saves and a 2.93 ERA in 15 games. He got six of the 13 first-place votes (Red Sox release, Nats Journal).
  • Tender day results (Nov. 20): The Nats non-tendered RHP Josiah Gray and lefties Carson Palmquist and Jared Simpson, and all three became free agents. Everyone else was tendered, including CJ Abrams, Jake Irvin and Jake Bird, who are all still on the 40-man (MLBTR, Nats 40-man).
  • Simpson’s very short vacation: Simpson was back three days later on a minor league deal with a spring training invite. RHP Marc Church (ex-Rangers) also signed one (MLBTR). RHP Luis Curvelo, whose fastball averages 95-plus, signed a minor league deal on Nov. 25 (MLBTR). Lefty Tom Cosgrove is reportedly returning on a minor league deal with a camp invite (MLBTR).
  • Where the DFA’d arms landed: The Mets claimed Gus Varland off waivers (MLBTR), and Braxton Roxby was traded to the Cubs for cash (MLBTR).
  • Jazz in D.C.? FanSided and The Athletic report the Nats have shown “real engagement” on free-agent infielder Jazz Chisholm Jr., who hit 19 homers and stole 41 bases for the Yankees this year. MLBTR says there’s no sign a deal is close and calls the fit “something of a surprise.” In the past five years, the Nats have only once paid a free agent more than $9 million a year (Nelson Cruz, $15 million in 2022) (MLBTR, NJ.com). The Athletic also reports Cleveland outbid Washington for lefty Kris Bubic (via RealGM). And Talk Nats says there have been no advanced talks with starter Michael King, who was Blake Butera’s college teammate at Boston College.
  • Turkeypalooza: The Nationals Philanthropies’ ninth annual Turkeypalooza handed out 1,500 turkeys and thousands of pounds of food to an estimated 15,000 community members, with help from James Wood (Nationals on X).

CBA Watch Weekly · CBA expires Dec. 1, 11:59 p.m. ET

Where it stands: There was a lot of activity this week, but no real progress. Both sides traded proposals, and neither moved on the big issue, the salary cap. Then the league took the union to court. The current deal expires at 11:59 p.m. ET Monday, Dec. 1 (10:59 p.m. CT), and owners are expected to lock out the players the moment it does (USA Today).

  • Nov. 20, small trades: MLB dropped its plan to keep high school players out of the draft. The union pitched changes to international signing but turned down an international draft (CBS timeline).
  • Nov. 23, new offers, same wall: MLB offered a $1 million minimum salary (now $780,000) plus new bonuses: $100,000 for pre-arbitration players, 5% for arbitration players who settle before a hearing, and $500,000 for one veteran per team. All of it depends on the union accepting a cap. The union pushed for more revenue sharing for small-market teams, including $20 million rewards for teams that contend, and agreed to split local TV money evenly as long as there’s no cap. The union’s Bruce Meyer said MLB’s offer doesn’t “add a single dollar” overall (CBS, USA Today).
  • Nov. 24, escrow dropped: MLB withdrew its plan to hold back part of player salaries in escrow and promised every guaranteed contract stays guaranteed. The union said the money would just come out of players’ pockets the next year instead (CBS).
  • Nov. 25, off to court: MLB filed a lawsuit in federal court in New York asking a judge to declare the coming lockout legal under antitrust law, even if the union dissolves itself (a “disclaimer of interest”) to sue. The suit names the eight players on the union’s executive subcommittee, including Tarik Skubal and Paul Skenes. The union called it “completely frivolous” (CBS, MLB.com, AP).
  • The key issues, in plain English: Salary cap and floor: MLB wants a $258.2M cap and $180.2M floor starting in 2028. The union has refused a cap since 1994. Revenue sharing: the union wants aid to low-revenue teams raised from about $700M to more than $950M, and MLB wants a 50-50 revenue split. Service time and free agency: both sides would let players 30 and older reach free agency after five years instead of six, but MLB ties that to the cap (USA Today).

Lockout odds: Very high. Federal Baseball puts it at “probably a 99% chance” (Federal Baseball). The good news is that no games are scheduled until Feb. 19, so a winter lockout doesn’t cost any games by itself. The last one ran 99 days, from Dec. 2, 2021, to March 10, 2022 (USA Today).

What it means for the Nats: Starting Dec. 2 the 40-man roster is frozen, with no signings, trades, waiver claims or releases. Team staff can’t even contact 40-man players, and Federal Baseball notes that includes trainers and holiday cards. Players will rely on offseason plans the team hands out by Dec. 1. Minor leaguers like Eli Willits and Seaver King can keep working with the team. Jarlin Susana and Travis Sykora can’t, because they’re on the 40-man (Federal Baseball, USA Today). The Winter Meetings (Dec. 7–9, San Diego) and the major league Rule 5 Draft are in doubt. In 2021 both were canceled.

Our take: If the Nats are going to add anyone else, like a starter or Chisholm, it has to happen by Monday night. Otherwise, settle in for a quiet winter.

#7Ronny Cruz, INF

Age 20 · Bats R · Fredericksburg (A) → Wilmington (A+) · 118 G · 15 HR · 52 SB · ETA 2029

Who he is: He was born in Santo Domingo and moved to Florida for his last two years of high school. The Cubs took him in the third round in 2024 and paid him $620,000, below slot. The Nats got him at the 2025 trade deadline in the Michael Soroka deal (MLB Pipeline).

2026: He tore up Low-A Fredericksburg for 14 games, hitting .333/.460/.627 with 3 HR and 15 steals, and was promoted in April. At High-A Wilmington he hit .220/.302/.367 with 12 HR, 18 doubles and 37 steals (caught 7 times) in 104 games, with 43 walks and 106 strikeouts. He was 19 for almost the whole season and turned 20 on Aug. 24 (MiLB.com, MLB Stats API). He played all over the infield at Wilmington: 41 games at short, 30 at third and 22 at second.

Tools and strengths: power and athleticism. MLB Pipeline says his exit velocities topped out around 110 mph and should keep rising as he fills out. He also has above-average speed and a plus arm. Pipeline grades: Hit 40, Power 55, Run 55, Arm 60, Field 55 (MLB Pipeline).

What needs work: plate discipline and contact. Pipeline says he’s still “swing-happy,” and the batting average shows it. There is progress, though. His walk rate in the 2025 complex league was 5.3%, and at Wilmington it was about 9.3%, with about a 23% strikeout rate (our math from MiLB stats). He also needs to settle on a position. The Nats are loaded with young infielders, but Pipeline says they plan to keep giving him priority reps on the infield.

ETA: 2029 (MLB Pipeline). Our take: Fifty-two steals and 15 homers as a teenager is a fun mix. If the contact catches up to the power, he could jump up this list fast.

#8Gavin Fien, OF

Age 19 · Bats R · Fredericksburg (A) · 76 G · .242/.337/.401 · 10 HR · 52 RBI · 44 BB · ETA 2029

Who he is: He was the 12th overall pick in 2025 by Texas out of Great Oak High in Temecula, Calif., and signed for $4.8 million. He was the 2024 High School All-American Game MVP and a Team USA standout. He was the headliner of the return in January’s MacKenzie Gore trade. His older brother Dylan is a catcher in the A’s system (MLB Pipeline).

2026: In his first full pro season, at 19, he hit .242/.337/.401 with 10 homers, 13 doubles and 52 RBI in 76 games at Low-A Fredericksburg. He walked 44 times and struck out 91 (MiLB.com, MLB Stats API). That’s about a 12.8% walk rate and a 26.5% strikeout rate (our math). Drafted as a shortstop, he moved to the outfield after an early-season wrist injury and played 39 games in center and 27 in right.

Tools and strengths: a patient approach, a quick right-handed swing, and a knack for lifting the ball, plus a plus arm that fits in right field. Pipeline thinks his 6-foot-3 frame could grow into plus power, and the Nats think he’s more athletic than scouts gave him credit for. Pipeline grades: Hit 45, Power 55, Run 50, Arm 60, Field 50 (MLB Pipeline).

What needs work: swing-and-miss, especially against sliders and changeups. Pipeline also says his unorthodox high-hands setup brings “some pause” about how it will hold up against better pitching. He’s an average runner at best and could slow down as he fills out.

ETA: 2029 (MLB Pipeline). Our take: A teenager walking that much in his first full season is a really good sign. If he cuts down the strikeouts at Wilmington next year, he could be a middle-of-the-order bat.

Ranks: MLB Pipeline Nationals Top 30 (checked Nov. 27). Next week: #9 Jackson Kent and #10 Miguel Sime Jr.

The NL East group chat Light ribbing, real numbers

Mets: They went big. Tarik Skubal agreed on Nov. 22 to nine years and $450 million, the richest deal ever for a pitcher. The Mets’ projected payroll was already over $340 million before they signed him. They also added Paul Goldschmidt on a one-year, $8 million deal (USA Today). And they claimed our old friend Varland, who had a 6.55 ERA in D.C. this year (MLBTR). Fair is fair: Skubal is a two-time Cy Young winner. Still, the Mets finished last in the East at 74-88 and scored 699 runs, 21st in MLB. The Nats scored 821, third in MLB (MLB Stats API).

Phillies: They made a multiyear offer to Japanese slugger Teruaki Sato, but he took six years and $120 million from the Cubs. The Phillies are one of three teams that have never signed a player from Japan’s top league to a major league contract (Inquirer, MLB.com). Their lineup scored 713 runs, 19th in MLB, the lowest of the Bryce Harper era, and it’s built around Harper (34), Kyle Schwarber (34), Trea Turner (33) and J.T. Realmuto (35) (Inquirer). They also needed a leverage lefty, and Puk went to D.C. (see Meme corner).

Our take: The Mets spent $450 million on one pitcher. The Nats spent $5 million on one and still outscored the Mets by 122 runs last year. We’ll call it even for now. 😄
