How the Nats can retake the NL East

Welcome

Welcome to the first issue of our family Nats newsletter! The big question this winter: what would it take to get from 77 wins to the top of the NL East? We dug into the 2026 numbers, the farm system and the offseason chatter to find out.

Stats from the MLB Stats API and Baseball Savant. Prospect ranks from MLB Pipeline's Nationals Top 30. Anything marked Our take is opinion.

The good news is that the Nats scored more runs than anyone in the division (821). They ranked 3rd in the NL in runs and homers (210), 4th in OPS (.744), and 1st in stolen bases (178). The problem was the other side of the ball. They allowed 815 runs, 189 more than Atlanta, and their 4.67 team ERA, 1.39 WHIP and 605 walks all ranked 13th or worse among 15 NL teams.

The bullpen was the biggest leak: a 4.99 ERA and 42 blown saves in 84 save chances. The rotation was better, at 4.37 ERA over 706.1 innings.

One more thing: the Nats finished 77-85 despite a +6 run differential. Our take: that usually points to close-game trouble, which fits a shaky bullpen, and it tends to even out.

Path back to the top Our take

  1. Fix the bullpen first. Converting half of those 42 blown saves would have flipped a lot of close games. Reliable late-inning arms are the cheapest wins on the board. MLB.com asked whether Jack Sinclair can be a late-inning option.
  2. Add one solid starter. Cade Cavalli (32 starts, 3.02 ERA, 169.2 IP, 200 K) is the anchor, and Andrew Alvarez (17 starts, 3.43 ERA) earned a long look. Connelly Early came over from Boston, where he made 17 starts with a 3.44 ERA, and debuted for the Nats with 3 scoreless innings. Jake Irvin (5.48 ERA in 20 starts) and Brad Lord are also on the 40-man. That's a young group, but one more proven arm would help. (Note: Foster Griffin, who had a 3.06 ERA in 2026, was traded to Cleveland on Aug. 3, so he's not part of the 2027 plan.) Ideas floating around include Yahoo's "Can In-House Option Upgrade Washington Nationals' 2027 Rotation?", SI's "Paul Toboni Hints at Who Nationals Will Have in Starting Rotation Next Season", and Federal Baseball's case that Tyler Mahle might be a perfect free agent fit (Mahle is not with the Nats; that piece is Federal Baseball's suggestion).
  3. Keep the offense young and running. The core is already here. James Wood hit .265/.388/.503 with 30 HR. CJ Abrams had 33 HR and 106 RBI, and SI reports the Nats are not ruling out Abrams staying at shortstop in 2027. Add Daylen Lile and Brady House and it's a young lineup. (Luis García Jr., who hit 23 HR, was traded to the Yankees on Aug. 3. The deal brought back four arms, including prospects Yovanny Cruz, Ben Grable and Jack Cebert.)
  4. Let the upper farm arrive. Seaver King, Jackson Kent and Luis Perales are knocking on the door (see below). Federal Baseball says the Nats have built a very deep farm system under Paul Toboni.
  5. Spend smart, not huge. Federal Baseball argues the Championship Series matchups show the Nats don't need a monster payroll to win. Targeted pitching adds over one splashy contract.

Our take: The Nats' lineup is five years younger than the Phillies' and three years younger than the Mets'. They also stole more than twice as many bases as the Mets (178 to 83) and 73 more than the Phillies. The Mets also trail on Savant sprint speed (27.08 to 27.75), so "slow" sticks for New York.

Fair is fair, though: the Phillies tied the Nats in average sprint speed at 27.75. They aren't slow, just older, and they don't run as much. We'll call them "veteran." 😄 And they finished 11 games ahead of us, so the young legs still have some catching up to do.
