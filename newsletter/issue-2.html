Buy or grow? The Nats’ big pitching question

Welcome back

Issue #2! It was a quiet week on the Nats’ transaction wire, but a loud one in the NL East rumor mill and in the Arizona desert, where our No. 4 prospect keeps breaking radar guns. This week: the big pitching question, a payroll scoreboard the Mets won’t enjoy, the NL East manager shuffle, real memes from X, and the Fall League report.

Stats from the MLB Stats API (through Oct 15). Sources are linked. Anything marked Our take is opinion.

Lead story: buy or grow?

The biggest Nats conversation this week was about arms. President of baseball operations Paul Toboni laid out the winter’s dilemma plainly:

“The offense has performed, so now it’s about moving the pitching group forward. The decision that we’re going to be faced with at times is going to be like, ‘OK, do we want to spend money on these players, even if it comes at the cost of the development of someone internally that we believe in?’”Paul Toboni, per The Athletic, via SI (Oct 15)

The money. The Nats opened 2026 with an $88.0 million payroll, 27th of 30 teams, according to USA Today’s list built from MLB Labor Relations figures. SI says an estimated $70.8 million for 2027 would rank last in the majors, and Toboni told MLB.com that “future payroll flexibility is a really good thing” (SI, Oct 10). Translation: there’s room to spend if they want to.

The “buy” case. SI’s Brad Wakai asks whether a top-of-the-market starter like Trevor Rogers or Freddy Peralta beats “adding a couple established bullpen arms” while the young arms develop. (Neither pitcher is with the Nats. Those are SI’s examples.) The need is real: MLB.com notes the 2026 Nats set a Major League record for blown saves.

The “grow” case. Exhibit A is #4 prospect Jarlin Susana. He opened the Arizona Fall League with a 103.8 mph fastball, the fastest pitch in the league since tracking began in 2015, and was named Pitcher of the Week. Toboni told MLB.com he has “physical traits that not many in the world have” and that the next step “becomes about how well could he fill up the zone.” Fair warning: Thursday was a reminder of that part. Against Mesa he allowed 5 earned runs in 2.1 innings with 2 walks, so his AFL line is now 8.0 IP, 6.75 ERA, 9 K, 4 BB in three starts. Jackson Kent and Luis Perales, who both pitched in D.C. this year, are also part of the “grow” pile.

Our take: Do both, in that order. Buy proven bullpen help and one sturdy starter, because that’s where the record-setting leaks were. Then let Susana, Kent and Perales fight for the jobs behind Cade Cavalli, Andrew Alvarez and Connelly Early. A 103.8 mph fastball is a lovely problem to have, but it’s not a 2027 plan by itself.

Quote of the week Real quote

From the same MLB.com story: while Susana was rehabbing in Florida, rehab coordinator Dan Mahoney told him to throw 97 to 99 mph “as you get loose.” Susana clocked 100-plus. Mahoney: “Jarlin, I’m telling you, you have to have it closer to 97. We don’t want to ramp it up this quickly.” Susana agreed. Then he threw triple digits again.

“[Susana] just turned to him and he’s like, ‘I’m trying. I’m trying.’”Paul Toboni, to MLB.com

Somewhere, every parent who has ever said “use your inside voice” feels seen. 😄

Our take: The Mets had the biggest payroll in baseball and paid $4.83 million per win, about 4.2 times what the Nats paid. They also finished three games behind us. That’s a lot of money for a last-place view of the division. The Phillies paid $3.22 million a win and went home in the Wild Card round.

Fair is fair: the Marlins got the best deal in the division at $0.99 million a win, so we can’t brag too loudly. And the Brewers, with $1.22 million a win, are the real blueprint. They won 103 games and just swept the Dodgers 4-0 in the NLCS, a team with a $322.4 million payroll. That’s the case Federal Baseball made last week that you don’t need a monster payroll to win, and this week proved it.

The NL East group chat Friendly trash talk

The division’s manager carousel took another spin this week.

  • Oct 14: The Phillies made Don Mattingly their full-time manager on a two-year deal through 2028. He took over as interim manager on April 28, when they were 9-19.
  • Oct 15: The Phillies hired former Mets manager Carlos Mendoza as their third-base and infield coach. The Mets fired Mendoza on June 26 with a 34-47 record (Reuters). So next year the Mets’ old manager will be waving Phillies runners home against the Mets. You can’t make this stuff up.

Our take: Mattingly made his big-league debut on Sept. 8, 1982. Our manager was born on Aug. 7, 1992, almost ten years later. Last issue we called the Phillies “veteran.” We stand by it. 😄

And this made us smile. Mattingly told MLB.com, “I want a club that plays faster. I think we have speed that we don’t use.” We know a team that used its speed: the Nats stole 178 bases in 2026 to the Phillies’ 105 and the Mets’ 83. Happy to send over the playbook, Don.

Fair is fair: Mattingly’s Phillies went 79-55 after that 9-19 start, the fourth-best record in the majors the rest of the way, per MLB.com. Then they scored 9 runs in three Wild Card games and lost to Atlanta. So the man can manage. We’re just saying our guy has a 31-year head start on the future.
